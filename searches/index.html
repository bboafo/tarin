The cedi and the dollar

Ghanaians search for the cedi when it moves fast

The four busiest months for ‘cedi’ searches were May 2025, December 2022, June 2025 and October 2022. All four are among the six months when the cedi moved most against the dollar: the dollar's price in cedis rose 27.7% in October 2022, fell 23.2% in December 2022 and fell 20.7% in May 2025. Month by month, searches and the size of the cedi's move go together (correlation 0.68 over 56 months, Bank of Ghana monthly averages). Searches show the moves get noticed; they can't say why the cedi moved.

Prices and fuel

‘fuel price’ draws the most searches about prices

‘fuel price’ is searched 3.8 times as often as ‘petrol price’, the next term in the group, and peaked in the week of 26 Apr 2026. ‘petrol price’, ‘food prices’, ‘price of rice’ and ‘transport fare’ are searched too little to read week to week; their monthly averages are on the chart. Power and water

Searches about power cuts have jumped

‘power outage’ searches over the last four weeks were 4.2 times their level in the year before. ‘ECG’ (the Electricity Company of Ghana), the group's most searched term, peaked in the week of 26 Apr 2026. In the IEA's May 2026 survey, electricity supply was the second most common reason people gave for disapproving of the president's performance (29.9%), after the economy: see the opinion polls.

Jobs and school

Searching for jobs and school

‘jobs’ is the most searched term in the group. ‘recruitment’ and ‘WASSCE results’ come in sharp bursts: their busiest weeks (16 Nov 2025 and 30 Aug 2026) were 6 and 15.5 times their five-year averages.

Method

Where the searches come from, and what they can't tell you

The data. Google Trends' “interest over time” for web searches in Ghana, weekly, for the past five years, in 4 downloads of five terms each. 100 is the busiest week for any term in its download, so terms compare within a group but not across groups. A value Google shows as “<1” is counted as 0.5. Google changed how it collects the data on 1 January 2022, so early-2022 steps may be partly that change.

How it's collected. Google Trends has no open API (its official one is an invite-only trial) and treats automated use of its website as scraping, so the files are downloaded by hand from trends.google.com about once a month. TARIN files each one byte for byte with its SHA-256 fingerprint and checks it before it is used. Each new download rescales the whole five years, so it replaces the last one; the old files stay archived.

What it can't tell you. Search interest is a share of all Google searches in Ghana, not a count of people, and it leaves out everyone who doesn't search online. A rise means more attention, not more worry or more support: people search for the cedi when it gains as well as when it falls. Terms in English miss searches in Twi, Ga, Ewe and other languages.

The checks. Every download goes through five rules before it is used:

    Downloads. trends-monthly.csv (monthly averages per term) and trends.json (everything on this page, with the weekly values and each file's fingerprint). Data source: Google Trends.
